Six Sigma: DMAIC & Process Control

A repeatable way to pick concrete mix components by what actually drives strength - plus the control charts behind statistical process improvement.

course
IMSE 4675 Six Sigma & Statistical Process Improvement
team
3-person team
timeline
Winter 2022
data
UCI Concrete Compressive Strength dataset (public)
DMAICMinitabRegressionControl chartsSPC

the idea

Concrete mix components are usually chosen by supplier stock and price. We built a material-selection process through DMAIC that ranks components by how much they drive compressive strength, so the choice follows the data.

DMAIC

  • Define: project charter and a process flow diagram of the selection process.
  • Measure: descriptive statistics, histograms, box plots, and normality probability plots in Minitab. Most components were roughly normal; age was exponential, so we ran the analysis with and without it.
  • Analyze: multiple regression with every component significant (p < 0.05) and R² of about 61%, falling to 44% without age - so age stayed in. Interaction terms added about 10% R² but were left out as unneeded complexity. Residual plots confirmed the normality assumption.
  • Improve: rank components by the size of their t-value - cement (24.82), age (20.99), blast furnace slag (17.39), then water and fly ash. Age can't be chosen, so the selection focuses on cement and slag: separate regressions for each, then an Excel comparison of the maximum and average predicted strength put cement first.
  • Control: we argued a selection procedure needs no ongoing control, but still built an I-MR (individuals and moving range) chart of compressive strength. Its out-of-control points stayed in, because no assignable cause could be found for them. Done again, I'd chart the strength of the batches the new process actually produces.

process control

The course also built the statistical process control toolkit: X-bar and R charts for variables, NP charts for defectives, moving range charts, and CUSUM charts to catch small sustained shifts faster than Shewhart limits do.

what it shows

  • DMAIC structure from charter to recommendation
  • Regression judgment - keeping the variable that matters, dropping complexity that doesn't
  • Honesty about what a phase can and can't do
