Lamp Plant Facility Layout

Turning a material-flow description into a two-floor plant layout - machines, operators, department placement, and a buy-or-lease call.

the case

Common Conduit, a lamp manufacturer in the course case, makes 13 of its 45 lamp variants at its Warren, Ohio plant in three product families: 3-way, decorative, and PAR reflector lamps. The lease on its three-story building was running out, it was looking at a two-story plant, and it wanted to add a part-time line to run 10 hours a day instead of 8. The question: lease or buy - and how should the new plant be laid out?

sizing the plant

  • Machines: F = SQ / (EHR) per department and product - standard time per unit, units per shift grossed up for 5% scrap at every operation, 90% operator performance, 480 available minutes, and 90% machine reliability - rounded up to whole machines.
  • Operators: total required time over available time per department, giving 12 operators - matching the plant's production headcount.

laying it out

The lamps are small, light, high-volume, and each family runs its own process, so we chose a group layout over a product or fixed layout.

  • Flow process diagrams for all three product families
  • A from-to chart of material moves between the 15 departments, ranked into a frequency table
  • AEIOUX closeness ratings - the count of A, E, I, and O relationships set at 5%, 10%, 15%, and 25% of the departments, the rest U - and the relationship chart built from them
  • SLP department placement starting from the department with the most "absolutely necessary" relationships: 8 - 9 - 2 - 1 - 5 - 6 - 10 - 11 - 13 - 12 - 7 - 4 - 3 - 14 - 15, across two floors with the larger, less connected departments upstairs and an elevator between them
  • Material handling: sortation conveyor, stackable containers, FIFO flow racks, forklifts, and a dock plan

the decision

We recommended buying: the plant fits an optimal department layout with room for employee services in a separate building - offices, cafeteria, nurse station - and open land to expand without another move.

my part

I wrote the introduction, objective, and executive summary, built the from-to chart and the flow and relationship chart with teammates, worked on department placement, and presented the flow process diagrams, relationship chart, and placement in our final presentation.
